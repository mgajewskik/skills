# Teardown

For anything that removes resources, data, access, or whole environments, however it is phrased. The deliverable is a staged removal: a reversible stage first, then a destroy the user executes against an explicit inventory, then evidence that exactly the intended things are gone and nothing else is. The destroy is always user-executed, in every environment.

1. **Name what must actually go.** The object, its cost, its exposure, or its management. "Stop managing" and "make unreachable" are often enough and reversible. Done when the user's goal is one sentence and the minimum removal that meets it is named.
2. **Build the inventory.** Per **[inventory-before-destroy](../principles/inventory-before-destroy/SKILL.md)**: a rerunnable read-only query resolves every selector to exact identifiers with count, owner, dependents and cascade behavior, live consumers, and retention obligations. Empty, unexpectedly large, or unstable results stop the playbook. Done when the inventory is saved as an artifact and its count matches the user's expectation.
3. **Establish recovery.** Per **[design-recovery-first](../principles/design-recovery-first/SKILL.md)**: what restores each target, evidence that the restore works for this data format, and what cannot come back (external effects, keys, identifiers others cached). Done when each target's recovery is `established` or the user has accepted `none` in writing.
4. **List protections.** Deletion protection, retain or prevent-destroy settings, finalizers, disruption budgets, and locks on the targets. A protection blocking the destroy is lifted by the user as its own explicit step, never bypassed by removing configuration or forcing.
5. **Snapshot the negative space.** Per **[verify-the-negative-space](../principles/verify-the-negative-space/SKILL.md)**: counts by type in the same scope and the named neighbours most likely to be hit by mistake, saved as an artifact.
6. **Run the reversible stage.** Per **[inventory-before-destroy](../principles/inventory-before-destroy/SKILL.md)**: disable, deny, scale to zero, detach, or stop, with its observation window and the signal that would reveal a hidden consumer. Outside production (or inside a grant) and reversible: log it with its revert and execute it. Otherwise: log it as a plan and hand it to the user. Verify it took effect.
7. **Observe the window.** Log any consumer error, access attempt, or alert. A hidden consumer means reverting the reversible stage from `revert-plan`, logged with `--reverts` (or handing that revert over), and returning to step 1 with the evidence.
8. **Hand over the destroy.** Log the destroy command as a plan for the user and give it to them with the inventory and the reversible stage's evidence from steps 2 to 7. The preview must show exactly the inventory's deletions and no replacements elsewhere. Large sets go in batches with a gate between them (**[bound-change-exposure](../principles/bound-change-exposure/SKILL.md)**). The inventory query is rerun right before execution and its count must still match, against the same identity, account, context, and state backend the inventory used (**[act-through-the-narrowest-identity](../principles/act-through-the-narrowest-identity/SKILL.md)**).
9. **Verify absence and collateral.** Each identifier is gone from the platform's own listing; cascaded dependents went as predicted; the negative-space diff shows only the approved identifiers; consumers are unaffected. Claims about saved cost or capacity follow **[explain-the-number](../principles/explain-the-number/SKILL.md)**. Log each claim as evidence.

**Reply.** What was removed (count and identifiers), stages with who executed each and their evidence, recovery status per target, negative-space result, and anything retained on purpose with its owner and revisit date.
